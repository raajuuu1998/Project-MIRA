About

Project MIRA is a computational pathology research initiative focused on learning clinically relevant molecular biomarkers from routine histopathology.

We study the intersection of histomorphology, molecular phenotype, spatial tissue organization, and representation learning, with particular emphasis on robustness across cancers, cohorts, institutions, and acquisition domains.

The broader goal is to develop biologically meaningful tumor representations that support transferable biomarker prediction and clinically relevant stratification.

Research Focus

Project MIRA examines how histologic morphology and spatial tissue context can support molecular biomarker inference while remaining robust to biological and technical variation.

Current methodological themes include molecular biomarker prediction, biology-informed representation learning, cross-cancer transfer, and domain adaptation and generalization.

Research

Project MIRA focuses on molecular biomarker learning from histopathology and the development of representations that remain biologically meaningful across cancer types and data domains.

Molecular Biomarker Prediction

Inferring clinically relevant molecular phenotypes directly from routine histopathology.

Cross-Cancer Transfer

Studying whether biomarker-associated representations remain useful across distinct tumor types and patient populations.

Biology-Informed Representations

Using morphology, nuclei, spatial organization, and tissue context to guide model representations.

Adaptation & Generalization

Developing models robust to cohort, institution, acquisition, and cancer-specific distribution shifts.
